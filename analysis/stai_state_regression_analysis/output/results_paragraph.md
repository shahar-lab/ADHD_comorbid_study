A Bayesian regression model indicated that participants with ADHD had higher State-Trait Anxiety Inventory - State (STAI-State) scores than participants without ADHD. The posterior distribution of the difference between groups (with ADHD − without ADHD) had a median of 9.70 STAI-State points, with a probability of direction (pd) of 100%, providing strong evidence for a positive group difference. Correspondingly, the posterior median of the STAI-State mean ratio (with ADHD mean / without ADHD mean) was approximately 1.64, with the ADHD group showing higher STAI-State scores, and a corresponding pd of 100%. The model-implied posterior distributions indicated a median of 9.1% of participants with ADHD (90% credible interval [7.1%, 11.7%]) and 1.4% of participants without ADHD (90% credible interval [0.9%, 2.2%]) estimated to score above the clinical STAI-State cutoff (STAI-State ≥ 40). Convergence diagnostics showed Rhat values of 1 for all examined parameters and high effective sample size (ESS) values, supporting good convergence of the model.
