A Bayesian regression model examined whether ICAR (International Cognitive Ability Resource) scores, a measure of general cognitive ability on which higher scores indicate better performance, differed between participants with ADHD and participants without ADHD. The posterior distribution of the difference between groups (with ADHD − without ADHD) had a median of -0.23 ICAR points, with a probability of direction (pd) of 73.58%, indicating weak and inconclusive evidence for a difference between groups and substantial posterior uncertainty about its direction. Correspondingly, the posterior median of the ICAR mean ratio (with ADHD mean / without ADHD mean) was approximately 0.97, with the same pd of 73.58%. No clinical cutoff applies to this measure, so a proportion of participants above a threshold was not estimated. Convergence diagnostics showed Rhat values of 1 for all examined parameters and high effective sample size (ESS) values, supporting good convergence of the model.
