A Bayesian regression model indicated that participants with ADHD had higher Prodromal Questionnaire-Brief (PQ-B) scores than participants without ADHD. The posterior distribution of the difference between groups (with ADHD − without ADHD) had a median of 2.43 PQ-B points, with a probability of direction (pd) of 100%, providing strong evidence for a positive group difference. Correspondingly, the posterior median of the PQ-B mean ratio (with ADHD mean / without ADHD mean) was approximately 2.06, with the ADHD group showing higher PQ-B scores, and a corresponding pd of 100%. The model-implied posterior distributions indicated a median of 25.9% of participants with ADHD (90% credible interval [21.7%, 30.4%]) and 9.1% of participants without ADHD (90% credible interval [6.9%, 11.7%]) estimated to score above the clinical PQ-B cutoff (PQ-B ≥ 7). Convergence diagnostics showed Rhat values of 1 for all examined parameters and high effective sample size (ESS) values, supporting good convergence of the model.
