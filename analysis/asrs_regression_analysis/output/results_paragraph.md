A Bayesian regression model indicated that participants with ADHD had higher Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS) scores than participants without ADHD. The posterior distribution of the difference between groups (with ADHD − without ADHD) had a median of 26.41 ASRS points, with a probability of direction (pd) of 100%, providing strong evidence for a positive group difference. Correspondingly, the posterior median of the ASRS mean ratio (with ADHD mean / without ADHD mean) was approximately 2.46, with the ADHD group showing higher ASRS scores, and a corresponding pd of 100%. The model-implied posterior distributions indicated a median of 70.2% of participants with ADHD (90% credible interval [66.0%, 74.2%]) and 0.5% of participants without ADHD (90% credible interval [0.3%, 0.9%]) estimated to score above the clinical ASRS cutoff (ASRS ≥ 40). Convergence diagnostics showed Rhat values of 1 for all examined parameters and high effective sample size (ESS) values, supporting good convergence of the model.
