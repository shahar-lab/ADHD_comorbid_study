A Bayesian regression model indicated that participants with ADHD had higher Beck Depression Inventory (BDI) scores than participants without ADHD. The posterior distribution of the difference between groups (with ADHD − without ADHD) had a median of 8.28 BDI points, with a probability of direction (pd) of 100%, providing strong evidence for a positive group difference. Correspondingly, the posterior median of the BDI mean ratio (with ADHD mean / without ADHD mean) was approximately 2.18, with the ADHD group showing higher BDI scores, and a corresponding pd of 100%. The model-implied posterior distributions indicated a median of 56.1% of participants with ADHD (90% credible interval [51.6%, 60.6%]) and 20.8% of participants without ADHD (90% credible interval [17.5%, 24.1%]) estimated to score above the clinical BDI cutoff (BDI ≥ 14). Convergence diagnostics showed Rhat values of 1 for all examined parameters and high effective sample size (ESS) values, supporting good convergence of the model.
