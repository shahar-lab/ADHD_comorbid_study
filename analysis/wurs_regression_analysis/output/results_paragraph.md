A Bayesian regression model indicated that participants with ADHD had higher Wender Utah Rating Scale (WURS) scores than participants without ADHD. The posterior distribution of the difference between groups (with ADHD − without ADHD) had a median of 32.03 WURS points, with a probability of direction (pd) of 100%, providing strong evidence for a positive group difference. Correspondingly, the posterior median of the WURS mean ratio (with ADHD mean / without ADHD mean) was approximately 3.49, with the ADHD group showing higher WURS scores, and a corresponding pd of 100%. The model-implied posterior distributions indicated a median of 72.9% of participants with ADHD (90% credible interval [68.8%, 76.7%]) and 5.6% of participants without ADHD (90% credible interval [4.2%, 7.5%]) estimated to score above the clinical WURS cutoff (WURS ≥ 36). Convergence diagnostics showed Rhat values of 1 for all examined parameters and high effective sample size (ESS) values, supporting good convergence of the model.
