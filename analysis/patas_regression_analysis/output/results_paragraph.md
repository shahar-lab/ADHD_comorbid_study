A Bayesian regression model indicated that participants with ADHD had higher PATAS scores than participants without ADHD. The posterior distribution of the difference between groups (with ADHD − without ADHD) had a median of 6.97 PATAS points, with a probability of direction (pd) of 99.92%, providing strong evidence for a positive group difference. Correspondingly, the posterior median of the PATAS mean ratio (with ADHD mean / without ADHD mean) was approximately 1.44, with the ADHD group showing higher PATAS scores, and a corresponding pd of 99.92%. No clinical cutoff has been established for this measure, so a proportion of participants above a clinical threshold was not estimated. This analysis was based on a smaller subsample than the other measures reported here, reflecting the more limited availability of PATAS data, which should be considered when interpreting the precision of these estimates. Convergence diagnostics showed Rhat values of 1 for all examined parameters and high effective sample size (ESS) values, supporting good convergence of the model.
