A Bayesian regression model indicated that participants with ADHD had higher Obsessive-Compulsive Inventory-Revised (OCI-R) scores than participants without ADHD. The posterior distribution of the difference between groups (with ADHD − without ADHD) had a median of 9.19 OCIR points, with a probability of direction (pd) of 100%, providing strong evidence for a positive group difference. Correspondingly, the posterior median of the OCIR mean ratio (with ADHD mean / without ADHD mean) was approximately 1.66, with the ADHD group showing higher OCIR scores, and a corresponding pd of 100%. The model-implied posterior distributions indicated a median of 56.9% of participants with ADHD (90% credible interval [52.4%, 61.4%]) and 27.2% of participants without ADHD (90% credible interval [23.6%, 31.1%]) estimated to score above the clinical OCIR cutoff (OCIR ≥ 21). Convergence diagnostics showed Rhat values of 1 for all examined parameters and high effective sample size (ESS) values, supporting good convergence of the model.
