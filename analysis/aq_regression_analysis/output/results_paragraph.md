A Bayesian regression model indicated that participants with ADHD had higher Autism Spectrum Quotient (AQ) scores than participants without ADHD. The posterior distribution of the difference between groups (with ADHD − without ADHD) had a median of 6.78 AQ points, with a probability of direction (pd) of 100%, providing strong evidence for a positive group difference. Correspondingly, the posterior median of the AQ mean ratio (with ADHD mean / without ADHD mean) was approximately 1.46, with the ADHD group showing higher AQ scores, and a corresponding pd of 100%. The model-implied posterior distributions indicated a median of 4.7% of participants with ADHD (90% credible interval [3.4%, 6.3%]) and 0.3% of participants without ADHD (90% credible interval [0.2%, 0.5%]) estimated to score above the clinical AQ cutoff (AQ ≥ 32). Convergence diagnostics showed Rhat values of 1 for all examined parameters and high effective sample size (ESS) values, supporting good convergence of the model.
